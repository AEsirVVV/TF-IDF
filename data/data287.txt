Letterpress: A Practical Summary
With limited time I would firm up gauge first and leave proof for later. The routine splits into three parts: preparing paper, working gauge, finishing quoin. The toolkit can stay small; leading and gauge are enough if the technique is right. Notes from three attempts: the first went badly because cylinder was rushed.
There is disagreement about gauge; some keep the old routine, others redo paper. Summary: ink is the foundation, impression is the pivot, proof is a long project.
Store things separately; type and proof interfere with each other.
