Field Notes: Bookbinding
In practice endpapers decides the outcome, while awl quietly shapes glue. A different stitching this time, and the first two days behaved differently. A friend suggests handling awl a day earlier for a more even result. This kind of judgement cannot be copied from a book; it comes from endpapers and press. Conclusion first: awl deserves more attention than endpapers at this stage. When progress stalls, revisit press; that step is usually the culprit.
Bookbinding rewards patience: hold endpapers steady and the rest follows. The toolkit can stay small; press and signatures are enough if the technique is right.
