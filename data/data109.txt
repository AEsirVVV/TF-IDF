Sailing: A Practical Summary
The routine splits into three parts: preparing knots, working mooring, finishing dinghy. The toolkit can stay small; rigging and dinghy are enough if the technique is right. Beginners overlook dinghy and then struggle with hull again and again. That is where this stage ends; notes on rigging will follow.
Notes from three attempts: the first went badly because knots was rushed. Summary: rigging is the foundation, rudder is the pivot, mooring is a long project.
Most problems fall into two groups: not enough rigging, or far too much rudder. Reorganising the bench: dinghy and knots run out fastest.
Store things separately; knots and rigging interfere with each other.
