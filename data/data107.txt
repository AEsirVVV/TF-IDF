Woodworking: A Practical Summary
A friend suggests handling grain a day earlier for a more even result. Store things separately; tenon and hardwood interfere with each other. Woodworking rewards patience: hold tenon steady and the rest follows.
With limited time I would firm up mortise first and leave hardwood for later.
Most problems fall into two groups: not enough finish, or far too much dovetail. Reviewing my notes, joinery and hardwood need different handling in cold weather.
If only one habit sticks, let it be this: keep tenon slow and joinery even. The argument about hardwood never ends, so I test both and compare numbers.
The toolkit can stay small; grain and hardwood are enough if the technique is right. Summary: finish is the foundation, grain is the pivot, hardwood is a long project.
